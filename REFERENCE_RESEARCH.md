## 2. Documented facts
- Artist: Ângelo Fernandes, Lisbon. Started 3D around 2013 in architectural visualisation, later moved to low-poly isometric work. (80 Level)
- Fishing Village: Behance lists Blender and Photoshop as tools, and Attribution / Non-commercial / No-Derivatives terms.
- His own description: he wanted a scene for exploring colour schemes, first considered a jungle or nature theme, and settled on the water village after trying several chromatic options.
- Modelling habits (80 Level): Loop Tools add-on, used only for its Circle option; Mirror modifier for symmetric objects; Array modifier for tiled or evenly spaced parts; no stacked "safety" modifiers; enjoys small props; aims for simple scenes carried by many varied props; gathers reference on Pinterest.
- Lighting (80 Level): three lights — a low-intensity top light as fill, a strong main light that creates shadow and contrast, and a weak light opposite the main to soften dark shadows. HDRI instead when rendering a single model.
- Software note (80 Level): Blender 2.83, with OptiX viewport denoising mentioned as helpful.

## 4. Analysis of the attached image (own observation)
**Composition.** Square water tile on a thick blue slab, seen from a high three-quarter orthographic-looking angle against a grey-violet studio backdrop with a soft vignette. The mass is asymmetric: village stacked upper-right, a long rock arm sweeping down-left and curling round to enclose a cove. The eye enters at the pale cove, is held by the gull flock in its centre, then climbs the ladders.

**Silhouette.** A leaning heap, wider at the top than the base, with roof corners breaking the outline everywhere and trees and palms as the crown. The undercut base and the cave mouths make the rock feel carved, not extruded.

**Architecture.** About two dozen one-room shacks, nearly all the same simple box with a mono-pitch roof — variety comes from rotation, height, colour and what is attached, not from different building types. Walls are vertical boards with visible patch planks nailed across. Roofs are corrugated sheets with overhangs, each carrying something: a basin, a block, a ladder.

**Layering.** Five or six terraces. Houses overlap in depth so that almost no wall is seen whole. Stilted timber platforms push out past the rock edge.

**Circulation.** Ladders dominate (a dozen or more); only two short stone stair runs; rope-and-post railings along terrace edges. Charming, but not walkable as drawn — a navigable version needs real stairs and ramps.

**Colour.** Warm terracotta rock occupies the middle values and unifies everything. Walls and roofs draw from a tight set: rose-red, pink, butter yellow, powder and cobalt blue, teal, lilac. Roof and wall on a house are usually complementary or split-complementary (pink roof on teal wall, blue roof on red wall). Greens are confined to foliage. Saturation is moderate; nothing is neon.

**Water.** Flat-shaded triangles in three or four blues with no transparency, no foam and almost no specular. It reads as cut paper.

**Light.** One warm key from upper left giving soft-edged shadows, strong ambient fill so shadow sides stay coloured, visible contact darkening under roofs and in caves. Looks path-traced.

**Storytelling.** Boat shed with sawhorses and stacked timber under the arch; two jetties with small boats; drying racks; laundry lines; potted plants on sills; life rings on walls; crates and sacks; gulls.

**Weaknesses worth improving on.** Little evidence of how people move between levels; no interiors; no night presence; buildings are one type.
